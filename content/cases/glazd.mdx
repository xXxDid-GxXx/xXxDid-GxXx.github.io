# Glazd

## Before there was a product (intro)

I moved to Poland from Ukraine, like a few million other people. The part nobody warns you about isn't the bureaucracy or the language. ==It's that you can't make friends.==

Not real ones. Not the kind where you end up in someone's kitchen at 2am arguing about something neither of you cares about. The apps don't help. Dating apps ==sort people by face, not by what's in their head==. Networking apps are worse: performative, full of people pretending to want coffee.

But loneliness was only half the problem.

## The other half (intro)

You can know someone for years, trust them, and still ==have no idea about half of who they are==. There are interests they never bring up because they think you wouldn't get it. Sides of them kept folded away because the moment never quite arrives. The same is true of you.

You both know you'd connect over the thing the other one loves, and neither of you brings it up, because the social cost of "here's a weirder part of me" is too high.

## The old internet

I wanted the old internet back. ICQ, AIM, forums, chat rooms where you'd talk to someone in another country for six months without knowing what they looked like. Somehow that was the most honest you'd ever been with anyone, because ==anonymity made it cheap to be weird==. Nothing was on the line.

Take away the face, the bio and the job title, and what's left is who you actually are. And sometimes the people you most want to be that open with are the ones you've known for years.

## The hypothesis

![Profile before any TikTok data is uploaded — the empty DOMINANCE.chart](/images/cases/glazd/profile-empty-state.png)

Around the same time I read that TikTok had become the app people trusted most. More than friends, more than therapists, more than their own taste. The algorithm knew them better than they knew themselves, and people said this with a kind of awe.

It clicked. When you scroll TikTok you're not curating or performing. You're just being. The videos you save, stop on, and share are data nobody edits, including you. ==It's the closest thing to a soul scan a tech company has ever built.==

So: what if you matched people on that instead of photos?

That became Glazd. ==Anonymous matching based on TikTok behaviour. No photos, no bios.== Pick any name. The other person only knows your city. Everything else you earn.

## Who built it

![Glazd Android welcome screen](/images/cases/glazd/android-welcome.webp)

Me and my best friend. He took engineering and the backend. I took product, design, and the founder-shaped work that doesn't have a name. ==We declared ourselves founders one afternoon and started building the next morning.==

It was the first time I used AI on something that was mine rather than a client's. The design came together fast, the idea felt solid, and the split worked: he was deep in data flows while I was sketching screens. We had momentum.

## The Google Play problem

![Discover screen — people online now, by interest](/images/cases/glazd/discover.webp)

Two weeks. That's how long Google Play takes to review a release, so every iteration on the testing track costs two weeks of calendar time. Which meant ==the MVP scope had to be ruthless==. We weren't shipping a finished product. We were shipping just enough to find out whether the idea was insane.

## The App Store problem

![The Glazd PWA running in a mobile browser](/images/cases/glazd/pwa-welcome.png)

iOS was the other half of the launch, and iOS is expensive. A developer account costs money, and the review process is slower and far more opinionated than Google's.

We were two people with an unvalidated idea. Paying for the App Store before knowing whether anyone wanted Glazd felt like setting cash on fire.

So we skipped it and ==shipped a progressive web app instead==. iOS users install it from the browser. It works offline and looks close to native. Not as good as a real app, but enough to test the idea on both platforms without doubling the launch cost.

The plan: once Google Play is live, TikTok access is real, and there's any signal people want this, we build the proper iOS app. ==Earn the App Store by proving it's worth it.==

## The TikTok problem

![Profile with the TikTok taste dominance chart](/images/cases/glazd/profile-taste-chart.webp)

The harder issue: we needed TikTok data, and TikTok doesn't hand out API keys to apps that haven't shipped. ==You can't get the keys without a live app, and you can't build the live app without the keys.==

The workaround was ugly. Users download their TikTok data manually and upload it to Glazd. Nobody was going to do that, and we knew it. It was a placeholder that let us ship something real to Google Play, so we could apply to TikTok with a working product to point at.

We released. ==First submission, approved.== Live on Google Play, PWA for everyone else. I built the TikTok API demo, sent it in, and got the answer: proceed to sandbox. Prove the integration there first, then we'll talk about production access.

## Where we are now

![User profile with match score and shared interests](/images/cases/glazd/match-profile.webp)

This is where the story stops being a triumph and becomes a real one.

My co-founder is a sailor. Actual ocean, actual ship. He went to sea, and the project paused. ==Not killed, paused.== The kind of pause where you don't open the repo for three months and feel guilty when you do.

The Google Play release is still live. The PWA is still live. The TikTok sandbox is still waiting. I plan to come back: finish the sandbox integration, get API access, run a real test cohort, and see if the hypothesis holds with real data.

Will I? Probably. We'll see.

## What I learned anyway

![Settings — safety, legal and privacy documents](/images/cases/glazd/settings.webp)

Even paused, Glazd taught me more than most shipped projects. How to scope an MVP when every iteration costs two weeks. When to choose a PWA over native. How to write a privacy policy that doesn't lie. How to design a look, pixelated, dark, old-internet, that tells you what the product is before you read a word. How to talk to platforms about API access. How to be a founder without the title.

And the thing I didn't expect: ==being honest about what you're building, instead of performing certainty, makes people more likely to download it==. The pitch was always "I don't know if this will work, but I want it to exist." That beat confidence.

## Naked, still

![An anonymous chat between two matched users](/images/cases/glazd/chat.webp)

Glazd is built on the idea that ==anonymity makes people more themselves==. The honest version of this case is the same bet. You don't get points for pretending it's a finished success. It isn't. It's a thing I made with a friend, frozen for now, that I might unfreeze, and that taught me more than most things in my career so far.

That's the case, and that's where it stands.
