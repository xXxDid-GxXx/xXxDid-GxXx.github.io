## Before we got there (intro)

A US tax review platform. The kind of software where someone sits down with a screen full of documents and decides, one by one, whether the numbers add up.

Before us they did this in Excel and folders. Real folders on a shared drive, with files called `TAX_REVIEW_FINAL_v3_REAL_v2.xlsx`. The pain was so obvious nobody bothered to explain it. They just shared their screen and let it speak.

The job: ==build the thing that replaces the folders==.

## Who was in the room (intro)

Three of us. A project manager who handled the client and the calls, me, and one other designer.

Both designers were in the company's top-10 AI initiative. We'd been using Claude and similar tools harder than anyone else internally, long enough that the company put us in a small group to work out what AI-first design looks like in practice. ==This project was where the theory got tested.==

It also turned out to be ==my last project as a regular designer==. Right after delivery I was promoted to product owner.

## The honeymoon

![Tax OCR main review screen](/images/cases/tax-ocr/main-screen.png)

The first week felt like cheating.

We'd talk through a problem, hand it to AI, get fifteen directions back within the hour, narrow to three, push further, narrow to one. ==Ideation that used to take days took an afternoon.== We were generating options faster than we could judge them.

I remember thinking: this is what people mean by 10x.

## The hangover

![The client design-system components rebuilt in Figma](/images/cases/tax-ocr/components.png)

The client wanted Figma. Of course they did. Every client wants Figma, with their design system, their components, their tokens.

AI gives you a mockup. The mockup is great. It's also a raster image, or HTML, or a screenshot in a slide. None of it is in Figma, and none of it knows the client's design system.

So you redraw it. Frame by frame, component by component. Every spacing token converted, every shadow rebuilt, every state built by hand in the client's library.

==We saved a week on ideation and spent it pushing pixels back into Figma.== It roughly broke even.

The honest version: AI didn't make us faster overall here. It made the good parts faster and the boring parts bigger, because more good ideas meant more translation work.

## The thing that was actually hard

![The document sidebar tree in its default open state](/images/cases/tax-ocr/default-open-state.png)

==The sidebar tree. That's where this case lived.==

Every tax review is a stack of documents in folders. Some downloaded, some pending. Some reviewed, some signed off, some both, some neither. Some force-signed without review, which is allowed and has its own status. And it depends on role: one person might be both reviewer and signer, or it might be two people, or the system might not know yet.

The sidebar had to show all of that without turning into the diagram that ate the user.

What worked was ==keeping status and hierarchy as separate axes==. The tree shows where things live. Status rides alongside. Roles get their own affordance instead of a fourth colour. Pending looks pending, not like a special kind of reviewed. Once we stopped encoding everything in one visual language, the tree became readable.

## What got built

![Bulk selection for review and sign-off](/images/cases/tax-ocr/bulk-selection.png)

Bulk actions for review and sign-off, because nobody wants to click four hundred documents one at a time.

A toolbar with the things tax reviewers actually use, stamps, zoom, a calculator, sitting where their hands already were.

And Work Paper AI: reviewers can ask the system about the document in front of them instead of cross-referencing six other tabs. The AI in this project lives in the product, not just in the process.

==We delivered in three or four weeks. It would have been six without AI.== The savings were real, just not where I expected: research and ideation collapsed, mockup execution didn't.

## The deeper truth

![The success screen after a completed review](/images/cases/tax-ocr/success-screen.png)

==You can't fake the deep work.==

AI gave us fifteen good directions. We still had to pick the right one, and the picking didn't get any easier. That's still the job, and whatever the next tool is, it won't get you out of looking at the problem and deciding what matters.

The honeymoon ends. The work resumes.

The client got their platform. The Excel folders died. And I got promoted out of being the person who redraws AI mockups in Figma, frame by frame.

So in a way, ==AI did get me out of the work. Just not how I expected.==
